1. Read the Pro number correctly

Pro is temporarily expensive because it is still a low-volume product in the old setup.

The earlier $0.51 / $0.59 discussion was the improved production-cost direction. The table below is landed cost and includes freight plus the current capacity limitations.

Why Pro looks high now

Pro is currently produced in lower quantity through the old 9-cavity Pro mold. It also uses more expensive raw materials and needs tighter control than Game/Eco. When the Pro quantity is low, the fixed factory overhead and handling cost are spread across fewer Pro pucks, so the landed cost looks heavy.

Why this is not a permanent Pro problem

Game/Eco is already close to the earlier $0.51 discussion when shown as landed cost at $0.54. Pro reduces as soon as the 25-cavity Pro mold and production flow are active, then reduces further at max capacity.

What happened in the earlier combined orders

In past orders, Pro and Game/Eco were produced together as one combined production cycle. During that period, the factory absorbed unexpected and previously uncalculated extra costs: urgent raw-material purchases, interruptions, corrections, trials, freight pressure and setup-related delays. Those costs made the combined production burden heavier than planned. The purpose of the remaining $75,000 is to move away from that emergency style and make the cost predictable.

The important message is: Pro is not permanently expensive. The current high landed cost is caused by low Pro output, expensive Pro materials and the old production bottleneck. The remaining $75,000 removes that bottleneck.
