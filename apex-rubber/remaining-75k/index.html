3. Channel strategy

Keep Ron. Do not build the factory around Ron-only pricing.

Relationship lane

Continue serving Ron because he supported the project early, but keep his pricing as a limited legacy lane.

Blend and mix control

Ron-style pricing should stay balanced. Pro-heavy orders at $0.66 need a separate quote or blended adjustment.

Growth channels

Use new capacity for team bulk, wholesale, distributor, printed puck, and stronger retail-facing channels.

Smart position to George: the remaining $75,000 is not being justified by Ron alone. It is justified because it moves the factory away from emergency economics and gives APEX the cost base to sell into better-priced channels.
4. Practical selling targets

APEX does not need full retail pricing to win.

The goal is not to sell at the highest public price. The goal is to sell above legacy pricing while still giving dealers and bulk buyers resale room.

Legacy lane$0.59 / $0.66

Useful for the relationship, but not enough to carry the growth plan.

Practical wholesale target$0.80–$1.05

Standard/Game target range that still sits below many public team/bulk prices.

Pro target range$0.95–$1.25+

More suitable for Pro because materials and quality control are heavier.

These targets are management targets derived from public market spacing, not guaranteed purchase orders. They are intentionally below retail/bulk public prices so the buyer still has resale margin.
Bottom line

Support Ron carefully, but sell the business through the market.

The remaining $75,000 makes legacy orders manageable and opens the real return path: stronger-priced bulk, team, distributor and printed puck channels. Ron can stay as a respected early-supporter lane, but he should not set the economics of the factory.
